AG learns backwards, not forwards. His own words (2026-09-13, forking-paths session): "just do it… while doing, you won't understand a thing. So read lesson nine. That will uncover another concept, so read lesson eight… a backward transition till you hit the atomic concept." It comes from his class-8 maths teacher, who started from the hardest circle theorem and asked for its predecessor, again and again, until "that's why it is called a circle". On 2026-09-14 he named it "my backward learning principle".

**Why:** Reading a paper front to back does not stick for him; he says he cannot retain or replay it. Claude has now proposed a forward, sitting-by-sitting reading plan THREE times (15-step derivation page 2026-09-07; "one step per sitting" 2026-09-08; eight-sitting plan for Kantamneni–Tegmark 2026-09-20) and each time it was the wrong fix. Claude itself said on 2026-09-13: "Reading a derivation was never the way you were going to learn this. Running it and breaking it is."

**How to apply:** Give him a concrete, runnable artifact FIRST (a plot, a number, a model output he produced). Let him say what he does not understand. Explain only that, one level down, then stop and let him ask the next "why". A paper is a reference to dip into from a question, never a syllabus. Do not write reading plans. He owns the questions ([[mathematics-curiosity-thread]]); keep explanations sourced ([[check-before-you-claim]]). He has asked for constructive framing of his own abilities — be honest, not flattering, and not negative either.
